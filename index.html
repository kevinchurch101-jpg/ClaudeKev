Course Intel — Sea Island, Seaside and Plantation

Seaside hosts three of the four rounds for anyone who makes the cut: par 70 and 7,157 yards, 152 longer than last year. It reopened on Oct. 19 after a Love Golf Design restoration. It is paspalum from tee to green in place of overseeded rye, with new TifEagle Bermuda greens cut back from about 7,200 to 5,940 square feet on average (Bamford), every bunker rebuilt, the 15th green moved to the water, the 16th stretched 34 yards and the 9th 28. Klos counts two par 4s under 420 yards where there were seven. Water or marsh touches 13 or 14 holes and 2.7% of tee shots draw a penalty, the fourth-highest rate on Tour (Sharpside), though the fairways run 37 to 47 yards wide and 70.5% of them get hit. Plantation is played once, Thursday or Friday: par 72 and 7,060 yards, tree-lined and sheltered, with four par 5s and three of them reachable. It has played about a stroke easier to par than Seaside over the first two days since 2016 (Klos); our files have it 1.3 easier on Thursday last year and 1.2 in 2024.

Eight inches of rain fell in October and more is coming. Haslbauer and Klos expected new greens to play firm; Bamford says sprigged greens will not bounce and the course is soft. We side with the rain. Winning scores on our files run from −16 (2024, in wind) to −29 (2023, in none), with cuts from −1 to −7. This week looks like 2024.

Approach zone and profile

Approach zone: 150 to 200 yards, with 100 to 150 second. Historically 63% of Seaside approaches came from inside 175 yards against 56% on Tour (Klos). With five fewer short par 4s, par 3s of 192 to 224 yards and a three-club wind, more of the week moves to the mid-irons. Each golfer's receipt prints his strokes gained per shot from both bands over the last 12 months.

The profile: an accurate driver who does not need length, gains with his mid-irons, and gets up and down when a gust moves the ball off a 5,900-square-foot green. Winners here have averaged 34th in driving distance and 23rd in accuracy (Rawlings).

What the writers say it rewards: approach (all of them); accuracy over distance and avoiding the big miss (Haslbauer, Coley, Sharpside, Rawlings); Bermuda putting (Rawlings, Bamford, Noonan); scrambling and wind pedigree this week in particular (Haslbauer, Coley); and current form (Jacobs, Tindall, and Rawlings, who notes four of the last five winners placed in one of their three starts before it). Engine: one run

Seed 20261118, 200,000 simulations. Form blends the 30-day, six-month and 12-month windows at .20 / .35 / .45, each scaled by rounds over rounds plus eight. The anchor is the player's own 12-month line shrunk by rounds over rounds plus 50. Fair odds: softmax with k = 1.875, fit to the market's no-vig. Favorite pre-check: Ben James −1.14 points on that layer and −0.34 on the simulation, inside the ±5 allowed. Simulation: whole-stroke rounds, scale 1.025, each player's own 12-month standard deviation shrunk toward the field median of 2.67 by rounds over rounds plus 50, cut at the top 65 and ties, ties share. The field's composite spread is 0.64 strokes a round (standard deviation), 0.61 on a neutral course. DataGolf's baseline and prediction are not in the composite and are not printed beside it.
